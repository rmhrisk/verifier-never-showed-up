## Note on the 1922 passport
This is a Belarusian People's Republic passport, chosen because it is a well-photographed
example of the post-1920 photographic booklet format. It illustrates the standard rather
than being an artifact of the League conference itself. Swap it if you want a strictly
League-issued document.

## Not included, and why
- **1837 English birth certificate** — GRO certificate images are Crown copyright with real
  reproduction restrictions, and web searches return mostly novelty templates.
- **1903 Prussian driving licence** — not available in any open collection found. Beware:
  the widely circulated "first driving licence" images are Karl Benz's 1888 authorisation,
  which is a different thing and would misrepresent the sentence.
- **Henry V safe-conduct, 1414** — survives as chancery enrolments in the National Archives
  (Treaty Rolls / French Rolls) rather than as a photogenic single document.

## Considered and not used
Available with clean licensing, but rejected because they decorate rather than argue:
- German nPA specimen card (public domain) and Estonian ID card (CC BY-SA 2.0). A photo of a
  card proves nothing the sentence does not already say. The object that *would* have argued
  something is the card reader German holders had to buy, and no clean image of one was found.
- Common Access Card example (public domain). Same problem.
- Estonian card reader (CC BY 2.0). Using an Estonian reader to illustrate German friction
  would be misleading.

## On the IDEMIA BORDERGUARD kiosk
Vendor product photography is copyrighted marketing material and is not reusable here.
The section 8 figure uses a public-domain US CBP photograph of the same category of
device, and the caption names IDEMIA, Thales and Vision-Box in text, which is fine.
If you specifically want the BORDERGUARD product shot, license it from IDEMIA directly
or request a press-kit image with written permission.

Also rejected: a CBP photograph of the O'Hare kiosk launch, because it is a press
conference showing an identifiable individual rather than the equipment.

The copy of this photograph circulating on kioskindustry.org is the same CBP image,
rehosted and reduced to an 8-bit colormapped PNG. The version used here is re-cropped
from the full-resolution CBP original via Wikimedia Commons, so provenance and licence
are verifiable rather than inherited from a third-party blog.
